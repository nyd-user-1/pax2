For the paper: Three rates are measured per paid run — the share of impressions that produced a 3-second play, the share of the video’s length the average viewer watched, and actions (shares, saves, comments) per play. Each rate is evidence-weighted toward the reference under thin delivery, expressed as a distance from the first round’s pooled average on the log-odds scale, mapped to a 0–100 percentile, and combined 25/40/35. The reference average is frozen, so later rounds are measured against the same yardstick.

For a 5th grader: A video has three jobs: make people stop, keep them watching, and make them care enough to do something. Each job gets a grade where 50 means “exactly average against the videos that came before.” If only a few people saw it, we don’t fully trust the grades yet, so they get pulled toward 50. Then the three grades combine into one — keeping people watching counts most — and that one number is Resonance.
