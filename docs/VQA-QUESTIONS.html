Video question bank — what the answer-from-measurement feature can ask

For the creator, about their own video

These run against the draft or the published file, answered with timestamps where the measurement allows.

  1. What is on screen in my first two seconds, and when does my first word land?
  2. Does my hook open on a face, on text, or on b-roll?
  3. Is there a call to action, when does it appear, and is it spoken, shown, or both?
  4. Are my captions burned in, and are they readable at phone size?
  5. What changes at the moment my loudness peaks?
  6. Does anything on screen contradict what is being said?
  7. Which of my two takes reads clearer with the sound off?
  8. How many distinct shots do I use, and where is the longest one?
  9. Is my thumbnail frame actually in the video?
  10. What would a viewer know by second three if they never unmuted?

For the partner, across their channel

Batch questions over a channel's set, answered as counts and lists with video links.

  1. Which of our shorts show the candidate speaking to camera, and which are b-roll only?
  2. Where does our logo or campaign branding appear, and for how many seconds per video?
  3. Which videos carry the paid-for-by disclaimer on screen, and which are missing it?
  4. Do our videos that outperformed 3x share a visual format?
  5. Which videos show an opponent, a news clip, or third-party footage?
  6. How many of our shorts open with on-screen text versus a person?
  7. Which videos show polling places, ballots, or voting footage we could reuse as b-roll?
  8. In which videos does the speaker appear within the first second?

For the platform (backfilling the census)

The FLAG-extract variables the census carries empty today; one batch pass fills them as measured columns.

  1. Captions burned in: yes or no (variable: captions)
  2. Call to action shown on screen: yes or no, with the timestamp (variable: callToAction)
  3. Format: talking head, graphic card, b-roll, rally footage, news clip, interview (new variable)
  4. Setting: indoor, outdoor, studio, archival (new variable)
  5. Number of people on screen and their screen share, counts only, per the no-identity rule
  6. Product, book, or merchandise shown: yes or no
  7. News chyron or third-party watermark present: yes or no
  8. Text density: share of frames carrying words, cross-checked against the OCR measurement
  9. Does the transcript match the on-screen captions where both exist (a QA pass)

As suggested prompts in the product

Rendered as tappable chips on a video page or in the chat's side panel, phrased in the reader's voice.

  1. What happens in the first two seconds?
  2. When should I ask viewers to act?
  3. What does this video look like on mute?
  4. Why might this one have outperformed?
  5. What is different about my best video?
  6. Show me every moment with on-screen text.

Where it lives

Three surfaces, one machinery; each one calls the same question-answering route with the video's measured record attached.

  1. A button on every video page that opens the chat in the side panel, preloaded with that video.
  2. Suggested-prompt chips in /chat when a video is attached from the plus menu.
  3. A page of its own later, when the question log shows which questions earn it.

From answer to recommended action

Every question resolves to a measured answer; the answer then takes exactly one of three paths.

Question
Measured answer
Grounded now
The variable already carries measured evidence. The recommendation renders with its citation: “Add an explicit ask: +0.59 points of vote choice (0.19–0.99).”
Earned by the backfill
A new column with no evidence yet. The batch pass fills it across 5,100 videos, the feature-lift formula measures its association with outperforming 3x, and only a measured lift mints the recommendation.
Stays descriptive
No association and no path to one. The answer renders alone: measured, no verdict yet. Nothing invented.

What makes it real

The tasks, in build order. Costs are EC2 boxes and one audit of human time.

  1. Stand up the answering model. One open video-language model on a GPU box; verified against 20 hand-checked videos before any answer is trusted. About one box-hour.
  2. Fix the question schema. Each census FLAG variable becomes a fixed question with a typed answer: 0/1, an enum, or a timestamp. One file, the single definition.
  3. Run the batch pass. The schema over all ~6,900 media files, answers landing in one generated JSON with coverage counts. A few box-hours.
  4. Audit a sample. 100 videos hand-checked against the model's answers; the measured accuracy rides with every column it produced.
  5. Merge the columns into the census and composition table, filling the FLAG·extract blanks.
  6. Measure each new column's lift with the existing feature-lift formula; record which columns clear a measured association.
  7. Write the recommendation registry: variable, grounds, citation text — the video page's existing recommendation pattern, generalized to one file.
  8. Serve the on-demand route: video record plus free question in, answer out, with per-tier limits.
  9. Ship the surfaces: the button on every video page opening the chat side panel preloaded, and the suggested-prompt chips.
  10. Join answers to the registry at render time: grounded recommendation with citation, or the honest “measured, no verdict yet.”
