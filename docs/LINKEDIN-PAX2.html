PostUpworthy ran the largest public randomized headline experiment there is, and its archive still teaches. pax2 Headline Ranker trains on 41,175 winner-loser pairs from 12,454 of those tests and picks the clicked headline 67.2% of the time held out, against 61.0% for a pattern-flag baseline and 50% chance. NYSgpt/pax2-feed-ranker

CardKnowing the reader lifts pooled AUC from 0.578 to 0.697 — and lowers within-reader AUC from 0.595 to 0.553. It tells readers apart, not what one reader will watch. Nothing serves it.

PostA negative result, published because it's the useful kind. pax2 Feed Ranker pretrains on KuaiRand's random exposures to ask whether reader history predicts which short a given reader watches through. Reader features lift pooled AUC from 0.578 to 0.697 — and lower within-reader AUC from 0.595 to 0.553. They identify who watches through more in general; they do not yet pick the video for the person, which is the question a feed answers. That 0.553 is the number to beat when this retrains on real reader rows.

NYSgpt/pax2-persuasion-reference

Card146 randomized ad studies, 28 coded traits: no trait recipe predicts an ad's effect in a study it wasn't fit on (leave-one-study-out R² of −0.021 to 0.003). Single traits carry measured differences. A reference; it scores nothing.

PostWhat doesn't predict is worth publishing too. Built from the replication archive of "How Experiments Help Campaigns Persuade Voters" (APSR 2024): across 146 randomized political ad studies, a model on 28 coded traits reads leave-one-study-out R² between −0.021 and 0.003. An ad's coded traits do not predict its persuasive effect out of study — the paper reaches the same conclusion. Single traits still carry measured differences: a plain-folks appeal sits +0.79 points on vote choice (95% interval 0.32 to 1.25). pax2 cites those differences beside a video's findings and scores nothing with the model.

NYSgpt/pax2-semantic-id-model

CardPLUM-style semantic IDs over 18,455 shorts. Failed its pre-registered gate on both allowed attempts, 0.558 and 0.571 against a 0.578 bar: the cells carry less than the continuous neighbors do.

PostFailed, recorded, published. Following PLUM's recipe (arXiv 2510.07784), 18,455 political Shorts were quantized into three-level semantic IDs, and each held-out short scored by its cell's record. The pre-registered bar: beat stock embeddings at 0.5775. Attempt one read 0.5584; the one allowed retry, with shrinkage, 0.5708. On the same shorts the encoder's continuous 20 nearest neighbors read 0.608 — at this corpus size, cells lose what neighbors keep. The quantizer itself is sound (0.862 normalized mutual information against the catalog's own clustering), and every table ships.

NYSgpt/pax2-resonance-encoder

CardAudience behavior trained into the embedding through a co-occurrence term: same-channel pair ranking 0.582 against the serving encoder's 0.562, ahead on six of seven held-out channels; gives back 0.003 of semantic kNN-AUC for it.

PostThe biggest ablation win in YouTube's PLUM paper was a co-occurrence term, so pax2 tested it at its own scale. pax2 Resonance Encoder adds 2,091 behavioral groups — same-channel co-outliers and measured co-surges — to the encoder's contrastive training. On held-out channels it ranks same-channel pairs at 0.582 against the serving encoder's 0.562 (bootstrap 95% interval +0.012 to +0.031, ahead on six of seven channels), and pays 0.003 of pooled semantic kNN-AUC for it. The trade is on the card, stated plainly.

NYSgpt/pax2-shorts-training-set

Card18,455 settled political YouTube Shorts from 64 channels, one document per short, each labeled against its own channel's median. The corpus every pax2 text model trains and benches on.

PostOne corpus, one label rule, every text model stands on it. 18,455 settled political YouTube Shorts from 64 channels — title, description, transcript — each labeled by its own channel's arithmetic: at least 3x the channel's median of settled shorts, 0.5x or less, or between; 14.8% clear the top bar. Ten channels are held out by a published rule, and no held-out short appears in any training pair. NYSgpt/pax2-outlier-benchmark

CardA frozen, channel-held-out test: does an encoder place a short near shorts that did as well as it did? The first two models it scored both failed their pre-registered gates, and the bar did not move.

PostA benchmark earns trust by saying no. This one freezes 3,869 Shorts from 10 held-out channels and asks one question of any text encoder: do a Short's 20 nearest training neighbors share its outcome? The first two models it scored both failed their pre-registered gates — the encoder missed its +0.02 pooled bar by 0.0101, the reranker fell below on both numbers — and the bar did not move; a later run cleared it at +0.031. Every number is computed twice, on the GPU box and again on a separate implementation, agreeing to four decimals.
https://huggingface.co/datasets/NYSgpt/pax2-outlier-benchmark
